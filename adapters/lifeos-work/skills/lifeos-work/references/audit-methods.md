# Audit Methods: AEO, Entity Authority, and Conversion/Consent

Method for the site, entity and conversion-path audit that LifeOS runs itself (`lifeos-work`, Plugin handoffs). `content-audit-strategy` is used only for what it covers: a whole-site content inventory from exports (prune, merge, update, keep). Facts about current platform and schema behavior (what is deprecated, what renders) are time-sensitive: **verify they are still current before citing them**, this space moves quickly. Business facts (entity names, addresses, phone, URLs, categories, constraints, conversion definition, jurisdictions, who implements) come from LifeOS entity data (`get_context`, `get_policy`, `search_context`), never from this file. Shared rules and the mode summary: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/references/core-rules.md`, Handoffs to installed plugins.

Contents: AEO extractability (site); the cited-but-never-named diagnosis and the entity work (consistency sweep, local listing, author identity, reviews, social); conversion and consent (capture inventory, tracking diagnosis, consent, conversion path).

## Mode 1 addendum: AEO extractability
Ranking and being cited are different problems: a page can rank first and never be quoted, because a retrieval system needs a self-contained, quotable passage, and a page written to hold a reader through a long scroll often has none. None of this conflicts with good SEO; most of it improves the page for a human reader too.

**Extractability checklist:** every substantive section opens with its answer in the first two or three sentences, true standing alone out of context (the single highest-leverage change on most pages) · the entity is named at first mention in the passage, not left as "we" or "the team" · headings are questions or plain statements of what follows, not clever phrasing · one idea per passage (a paragraph covering three things cannot be quoted for any of them) · specifics are stated, not gestured at (a dated figure, not "prices vary") · a current date is visible in or beside the passage (where LifeOS entity policy allows dates in content) · definitions appear before they are used · tables for comparisons and thresholds, which survive extraction better than the same content in prose · no answer buried behind narrative scene-setting (a personal-story opening before the substance is the most common cause of a page that ranks and is never cited).

**Length is not the lever:** word count correlates poorly with citation rate in independent studies; write the length the topic needs, without padding for citability and without shortening a good page.

**Markup guidance (verify current before acting):** structured data for a rich-result type that has since been deprecated does no harm on existing pages but is not added to new ones, and removing it is not itself a task; check the platform's current documentation before recommending any specific schema addition or removal. Self-serving review-star markup generally does not render for local-business or organization entity types however it is injected; where a business's reviews live entirely on third-party platforms, record that constraint instead of recommending markup that cannot be substantiated. No schema is required to appear in AI features, and structured data must match visible page text: schema is a machine-readability layer for entity resolution, not a ranking lever.

**Technical:** content in server-rendered HTML (anything needing JavaScript execution may not be seen by a retrieval crawler) · check `robots.txt` for AI-crawler directives and confirm the current setting matches what the business intends (blocking them is a legitimate decision with a real AEO cost either way) · fast, unauthenticated, uninterrupted access (a cookie wall or interstitial can block retrieval entirely, and a content-blocking consent banner is an AEO risk as well as a conversion one) · clean semantic HTML with real headings and lists, not styled divs (a common page-builder failure mode).

**An `llms.txt` file's practical value for AI-search visibility is unconfirmed and adoption is low:** no audit work is scheduled around it for visibility reasons; where one exists and is auto-generated, it stays generated.

**Limits of the claim:** no single change is promised to improve citation (these systems are non-deterministic and selection criteria are undisclosed; the honest framing is "easier to quote, better odds"). Nothing is written for the machine at the reader's expense. AEO is not separate from content quality: a page with nothing worth quoting cannot be made citable by formatting alone. A naming failure (below) is not a page problem: if pages are already being cited, nothing is blocked there.

## Mode 2 addendum: the cited-but-never-named diagnosis
Run whenever the AI-visibility panel (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/references/scoring-and-verdict-framework.md`) shows citations without the business's name appearing. **What is happening:** AI systems cite pages by retrieving them from a live index, and name brands from an internal entity representation: two separate mechanisms. A business can be quoted constantly while never named, because it has not resolved as a distinct entity. **What does not fix it:** more content, more markup, more citations, anything at the page level.

**Four workstreams, in order:**
1. **One canonical entity:** a single organization representation with a stable identifier, and a person entity for any named individual (with its own identifier, profile links and area of expertise), linked to the organization. Values match LifeOS entity data exactly.
2. **Resolving cross-references:** every owned or claimed property listed and pointing at profiles that resolve back. This is the most direct statement of the entity graph available, and it is usually incomplete or missing.
3. **One naming convention everywhere:** every variant name in public use splits one entity's authority across things the system does not know are the same; every variant found is a task.
4. **Third-party corroboration:** most brand mentions in AI answers come from third-party pages, not the business's own site; within audit scope that means directory and profile completeness, review platforms and press mentions carrying correct details, not outreach or link building.

**The proof target is the business resolving as a distinct, named entity** (a knowledge-panel equivalent, where the platform has one), measured by the "named" side of the AI-visibility panel moving. Expect nothing for roughly three months: this is slow, compounding work, not a quick fix.

**The consistency sweep:** one row per property, one column per entity fact (business name, address, phone, website, category, links out to), filled from what is actually live. Include the website itself (footer, contact page, schema), the primary local-listing platform, review platforms, industry and professional directories, every social profile, video and podcast profiles, and any press mention carrying a business detail. **Mismatch types:** any address off the publishable list, suite or unit notation dropped or inconsistent, a registered name abbreviated or a legally required element dropped, a role or status described incorrectly, a phone number differing in any digit or format, a URL differing in protocol or subdomain, a category that misdescribes the business. **Group fixes by property, not by field**, so the operator does one login and fixes everything there at once. Each mismatch is one line: `Property: [name/URL] · Field: [exact field] · Currently: [exact live value] · Change to: [exact correct value from LifeOS entity data] · Access: [login, or copy-supply] · Effort: [minutes]`. Properties marked Future or Paused in LifeOS entity data are never audited or scored but still enter the sweep.

**Primary local-listing checklist** (a map-listing profile or the market's equivalent): **confirm what exists before recommending anything**, since creating a duplicate is worse than having none and is hard to undo · claimed and verified · name matching real-world signage exactly, no keyword stuffing (risks suspension) · address and phone matching LifeOS entity data exactly · category correct · service areas set where relevant · hours, description and attributes complete (empty fields are lost signal for no effort) · photos current · website link pointing where the business wants traffic · reviews answered with the right signature · public Q&A reviewed (often editable by anyone and frequently carries wrong information nobody has noticed).

**Author identity** (extra rigor for any content touching high-stakes decisions: health, financial, legal, safety): a named human author where LifeOS entity data calls for one, never an agency or team name standing in as a person · an author page with real credentials, role and any license or certification status, consistent across pages · a genuine published date and a genuine modified date (**never bulk-update dates on content that was not revised**) · the business's own name in body text, not only the footer (the on-page half of the naming fix) · geographic and service scope stated in plain retrievable text, not only in a map embed.

**Reviews as signal:** every review-platform profile complete, correct and linked from the site · platforms present in the cross-reference list above · where volume and ratings are strong, they appear on-site as content with honest attribution of where the review lives · no rating or review markup where the markup guidance above says it will not render · no claim published that the underlying data does not support.

**Social and profile optimization** (profile level only; posting strategy is out of scope): bio and about fields carry the correct business name, service area and one clear statement of what the business does · link fields point somewhere current · handles, display names and imagery consistent enough to read as one entity · dormant profiles identified (an abandoned profile with stale details is an active liability; the choice is update or close it) · profiles cross-linked where the platform permits.

## Mode 3 addendum: conversion and consent, in full
**If a lead cannot be attributed to a page, nothing here can be proven to have worked, every lead figure is modelled, and the business has no evidence for the work.** This is why measurement outranks nearly everything else in sequencing, and tracking measures a path that has to exist first.

**The capture inventory:** list every route a lead can take, per property: contact or enquiry forms per template, phone links, messaging-app links, email links, booking or scheduling embeds, third-party widgets, and any offline route that leaves no digital trace (noted for honesty although it cannot be tracked). For each: which pages carry it, whether it is visible without scrolling on mobile, whether it works on a real handset, and whether it fires anything measurable. **Test the phone or contact link on a real device**: a call button hidden by a responsive layout setting, or a number that fails to dial correctly from outside the business's home region, are both invisible in every analytics tool.

**The tracking diagnosis: establish which state applies before recommending anything.**
1. No tag manager, no conversion events: nothing attributable; everything else is downstream of this.
2. Tag manager installed, no events configured: fast fix, high value.
3. Events configured but firing incorrectly or incompletely: the most dangerous state, because the data looks real and is not; verify by triggering each event and watching it arrive.
4. Events correct, no CRM connection: enquiries are counted, quality unknown; workable, and named as a limitation in every report.

**Recommend in this order:** (1) install a tag manager if none exists; (2) configure the events that matter for the business's conversion definition, named consistently, each carrying the page path as a parameter (link-click triggers for links, element triggers for injected buttons, a data-layer push for anything inside an iframe); (3) mark them as key or conversion events in the analytics platform, or they will not surface where the business looks; (4) verify each by actually triggering it (an event configured and never verified produces confidently wrong reporting for months); (5) redact personal data: never send a raw phone number or email as an event parameter; (6) only then connect a CRM (lead source in the CRM closes the loop between an enquiry and its quality, which changes prioritization more than any other single data point). **Route alternatives honestly:** some events can be captured without a tag manager and some cannot; say which is which instead of presenting a partial workaround as a full solution.

**Consent and jurisdiction: a researched, not assumed, method** (the same research-and-confirm method as the approved-sources list, the add-client recipe of the `lifeos-entity` skill): identify which privacy and consent regimes apply to the business's real markets (from LifeOS entity data jurisdiction facts) and state a defensible baseline for that set. **Flag plainly that this needs confirmation from a qualified privacy professional in that jurisdiction, and never state a legal requirement as settled in a client-facing document.** Audit for: whether a consent mechanism exists at all, whether any advertising pixel runs without one, whether the privacy notice describes what is actually collected, and whether a banner blocks content on mobile (a conversion and AEO problem as well as a legal one). **Where an advertising pixel is live with no consent mechanism, that is a Critical finding that gates further tracking work.**

**Conversion path checklist:** every page with real traffic offers a route to contact without scrolling on mobile · forms ask only for what the enquiry requires (every extra field costs completions) · the call to action is specific to the page's topic, not a generic default · a submission produces a visible confirmation, and the tracking event fires on that confirmation · contact details match LifeOS entity data everywhere (**a wrong number or address is a total conversion failure and is invisible in every SEO tool**) · one canonical contact number and address everywhere (dynamic number insertion or call-tracking swaps fight the entity-consistency work directly; keep the canonical value in crawlable HTML and in schema) · any consent or disclaimer wording required in the jurisdiction is present (where the business has its own compliance policy in LifeOS entity data, that policy owns the wording and the audit only flags absence).
